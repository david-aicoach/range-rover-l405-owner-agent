# Cooling-system triage — L405 owner questions

## Low-coolant warning that appears then disappears
Rank these first unless evidence points elsewhere:
1. **Actual coolant level near the low threshold.** Movement, temperature change or vehicle attitude can make a marginal level trigger intermittently.
2. **Expansion-tank level sensor/float issue.** A sticking or faulty level sensor can report low coolant when the visible cold level is correct.
3. **Small coolant leak.** Slow leaks can leave little evidence on the ground because coolant may evaporate on hot components or collect in undertrays.

Do not declare any one cause proven until the cold level and leak history are checked.

## Immediate owner check
- Let the engine become fully cold.
- Inspect the translucent expansion tank against its cold-fill/min-max marking.
- Never open a pressurised coolant cap when hot.
- Look for dried pink/orange/white residue, dampness, sweet smell, wet undertray, steam, or repeated level reduction.

## If the level is below minimum
Treat this as real coolant loss until proven otherwise. Top-up advice must use the correct JLR coolant specification for the vehicle/market; if engine/VIN is unknown, do not guess the exact fluid.

## If the cold level is correct
A level sensor/float or electrical issue moves higher on the list, but pressure-test the cooling system if the warning repeats.

## Stop-driving triggers
Advise the driver to stop safely and switch off if any of these occur:
- temperature gauge/overheat warning rises abnormally;
- steam is visible;
- coolant is rapidly disappearing;
- heater suddenly blows cold while engine temperature rises;
- engine runs roughly after overheating;
- large leak or coolant smell with visible vapour.

## High-value workshop verification
1. Cooling-system pressure test cold and, if needed, hot.
2. Inspect expansion tank/cap and level sensor operation.
3. Inspect engine-specific crossover pipes, hoses, thermostat/water-pump area and radiator connections.
4. Scan for stored cooling-system or temperature-related DTCs with JLR-capable diagnostics.
5. If unexplained loss persists, test for combustion gases / internal leak as appropriate.

## Evidence language
Say "common/credible suspect" rather than "known failure" unless a JLR bulletin, campaign or robust multi-source evidence is available for the exact engine/build.
